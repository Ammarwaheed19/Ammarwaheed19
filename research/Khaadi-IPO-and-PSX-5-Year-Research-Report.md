# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Khaadi IPO: bottom line

* **What is being sold [F]:** 500mn shares (20% of post-IPO capital) of **Rs0.125 face value**. 250mn are a pre-IPO placement and 250mn are offered through book building with a **floor of Rs12.40** and a **ceiling of Rs18.00** (+45.16%). The pre-IPO price is set at (floor + strike)/2, capped at Rs15.20. Total raise is **Rs6.2bn–8.3bn**. Arif Habib Limited is lead manager. Book building is expected in November 2026. ([PSX notice](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/themes/psx/uploads/Notice-for-seeking-public-comments-Khaadi.pdf), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-01/pakistan-s-fashion-brand-khaadi-plans-ipo-next-month), [Mettis](https://mettisglobal.news/Khaadi-plans-to-raise-up-to-Rs83bn-through-preIPO-IPO-63907))
* **Implied valuation [C]:** 2,500mn post-IPO shares × price gives **Rs31.0bn (floor) to Rs45.0bn (ceiling)** market cap, about US$112–162mn at ~Rs278/US$.
* **The headline "6.5x P/E" is computed at the floor price on management's CY2027 *forecast*** (Rs4.8bn PAT ÷ 2.5bn shares = Rs1.92 EPS; 12.40 ÷ 1.92 = 6.5x) **[C]**. On **actual CY2025 profit (Rs1,668mn)** the same IPO is priced at **18.6x (floor) to 27.0x (ceiling)** **[C]**. The market trades at about 7–8x forward. The forecast needs PAT to grow **2.9x in two years** (+86% in CY26, then +55% in CY27). Revenue grew only **+2.2% in CY2025** **[C]**.
* **The Rs0.125 face value is NOT a loophole [O].** It is a capital-structure choice with zero effect on intrinsic value. At a Rs10 face value the same deal would be priced at **Rs992–1,440 per share** for 31.25mn shares, which is economically identical. One real but minor issue: Pakistani dividends are quoted as "% of face value", so a "100% dividend" from Khaadi is only **Rs0.125/share**, which is easy for retail investors to misread.
* **The real governance concerns lie elsewhere [O]:**
  1. **The listed company does not own the Khaadi brand.** IP sits with the parent, Weaves Corporation Limited (WCL), under a *Brand Usage Rights (BUR) agreement* that also allocates "centralized group costs" **[F]**.
  2. **International operations (UAE/UK/US) are NOT in the listed company.** They sit in other Weaves group companies, while Khaadi Pakistan *supplies* them. That creates related-party transfer-pricing exposure **[F]**.
  3. Gross margin jumped from **~27–30% (FY23 / 6M Dec-23) to 34–37% (CY24–1H26)** around the time of a year-end change and group renaming. The cause is n.v. and needs explaining.
  4. **Store count fell from 41 (2024) to 31 (2026)** **[F]** while nominal revenue was flat in a ~20%+ inflation period, so **real revenue declined**.
  5. **Sapphire Retail, a direct competitor, had FY25 revenue of Rs41.4bn growing +26%** ([PACRA](https://pacra.com/api/rating-report/MTUxOTY=)), against Khaadi's Rs25.1bn growing +2%. Khaadi is no longer the category's growth leader.
* **My valuation range [O/C]:** fair value about **Rs13.5–16.0** (central ~Rs14.5), based on 8.5–10.5x my *normalised* CY27 EPS of Rs1.52 (Rs3.8bn PAT, below management's Rs4.8bn).
  * **Attractive ≤ Rs13.5** · **Fair Rs13.5–16** · **Expensive > Rs16** · **Avoid ≥ Rs17 unless 3Q/4Q CY26 results confirm management's run-rate.**
* **2031 scenarios [C/A]:** bear **Rs8.5–9.9**, base **Rs20.5–25.7**, bull **Rs41–48**. From a Rs18 strike, the base case returns only **~5% a year price-only (~9% with dividends)**, below a Pakistani T-bill. From Rs12.40 it returns **~13% (~17% with dividends)**.
* **Recent PSX IPO evidence [F/C]:** the three hottest 2026 IPOs priced at or near the **top of their bands after 3–7x oversubscription** (Ghani Dairies, Wahdat Poultry, Sitara Petroleum) trade **14–46% below issue**. Over ~5 years, **none of the six 2020–21 IPOs I could price beat the KSE-100**. The index rose ~+275% from end-2021; the best IPO rose +105%.

## 1B. Primary or secondary? Where does every rupee go?

**Evidence the whole issue is primary (new shares) [F→O]:**
* The notice says "**the Company** … signed agreements to sell" the pre-IPO shares. The seller is the issuer, not WCL.
* The 500mn shares are described as "20% of the **post-IPO** paid-up capital", the language of a capital increase.
* The IPO-tranche use-of-proceeds table adds up exactly to 250mn × Rs12.40 = **Rs3,100mn**: Rs1,764mn (57%) bridge repayment + Rs1,336mn (43%) expansion **[F/C]**.

**Caveat:** press reports said the pre-IPO shares "have been placed with blue-chip investors" ([Nukta](https://nukta.com/khaadi-plans-to-raise-up-to-pkr-8-3-billion-ahead-of-psx-listing)), but the notice says only that agreements were signed. **What the pre-IPO cash will be used for is n.v.** It could fund the planned ~Rs3.5bn/yr capex from 2027 ([Mettis](https://mettisglobal.news/Khaadi-plans-to-raise-up-to-Rs83bn-through-preIPO-IPO-63907)). **If the final prospectus shows any pre-IPO shares being *sold by WCL*, that money goes to the parent, not the company, and my analysis changes. This is the first thing to check.**

**In plain language: where does every rupee go? [O]**
* About **Rs1.76bn simply repays bank loans** already used to build 8 Experience Stores. That money has already been spent; the IPO refinances it.
* Rs1.34bn (at the floor) pays for the remaining fit-out of those stores. The 8 stores cost **Rs3,387mn in total**, of which **Rs2,051mn was already spent by 1H CY26**: Rs2,247mn shop fit-out, Rs771mn furniture/fittings, Rs370mn equipment **[F]**.
* The remaining ~Rs3.1–5.2bn (the pre-IPO cash plus any premium above the floor) has **no use I could verify**. Most likely it funds future capex and working capital.
* **Nothing goes to the sponsor *if* the issue is fully primary.** WCL gets a listed 80% stake valued at Rs24.8–36.0bn (pre-money) [C].

|---|---|---|
| Pre-IPO investors | (12.40 + S)/2 | Rs15.20 → **15.6% cheaper** |

## 2B. Testing the loophole theory: trying to DISPROVE it first

**Hypothesis:** "A Rs0.125 face value lets insiders sell shares at Rs18, a 144x premium to par, so retail investors are being exploited."

**Test 1: Does a split change anyone's ownership? No [C].**
Suppose WCL owned 25mn shares at Rs10 (Rs250mn capital) and splits 80:1. It then owns 2,000mn shares at Rs0.125, still Rs250mn capital and still 100%. Nothing changes except the number of pieces.

**Test 2: Does a bonus issue change value? No [C].**
A bonus issue moves reserves into share capital. Equity, assets, earnings and ownership percentages are unchanged. Per-share figures (EPS, BVPS) fall in proportion to the higher share count.

**Test 3: Does par value affect the IPO valuation? No [C].** Rebase the same deal to a Rs10 face value (÷80 shares, ×80 price):

| | Actual (Rs0.125) | Rs10-equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Post-IPO shares | 2,500mn | 31.25mn |
| IPO price band | Rs12.40–18.00 | **Rs992–1,440** |
| CY25 EPS | Rs0.667 | Rs53.4 |
| Market cap at floor / ceiling | Rs31.0bn / Rs45.0bn | **identical** |
| P/E on CY25 at floor / ceiling | 18.6x / 27.0x | **identical** |
| Premium over par at floor | 99.2x par | 99.2x par |

**Test 4: Is "premium over face value" economically meaningful? No [O].** Compare price ÷ par for well-known PSX stocks [C]: SAZEW ≈ Rs1,600/Rs10 = **160x**, LUCK ≈ Rs411/Rs2 = **205x**, MEBL ≈ Rs548/Rs10 = **55x**, FFC ≈ Rs538/Rs10 = **54x**. Khaadi at Rs12.40–18 is **99–144x**. Par value is an accounting label. The only valuation questions that matter are **P/E, EV/EBIT, P/B, cash flow and growth.**

**Test 5: Is a sub-Rs10 par legal and done before? Yes [F].** The Companies Act 2017 (s.85) allows subdivision. Recent PSX examples:
* Lucky Cement Rs10→Rs2 (5-for-1, Apr 2025): [BR](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40349302/stock-split-lucky-cement-approves-sub-division-to-enhance-investor-participation), [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/04/15/lucky-cement-to-suspend-trading-from-april-21-to-implement-15-stock-split/)
* Systems Ltd Rs10→Rs2: [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/03/25/systems-limited-to-undergo-5-for-1-stock-split-to-enhance-liquidity/amp/)
* UBL Rs10→Rs5: [Mettis](https://mettisglobal.news/ubl-to-subdivide-shares-cutting-face-value-from-rs10-to-rs5/)
* Lucky Core Rs10→Rs2: [Mettis](https://mettisglobal.news/lucky-core-board-approves-share-face-value-sub-division/)
* SPEL Rs10→Rs5: [Mettis](https://mettisglobal.news/SPEL-to-sub-divide-its-share-face-value-from-Rs10-to-Rs5-each)
* Hum Network Re1: [SCS](https://www.scstrade.com/stockscreening/SS_CompanySnapShot.aspx?symbol=HUMNL)
* K-Electric Rs3.50: [invest.pk](https://invest.pk/symbol/stock/KEL)
* **Tasdeeq IPO (Aug 2026) at Re1 par, priced at Rs3.00**: [BR](https://www.brecorder.com/news/amp/40433630)

**Conclusion of the loophole test [O]:** the theory **fails**. The Rs0.125 face value transfers no value, creates no extra shares for insiders beyond what the split proportionally creates for the sole existing shareholder, and does not change any valuation multiple. **Khaadi's Rs0.125 is unusually low.** I found no other PSX company below Re1 par (n.v. as an exhaustive claim). But it is unusual in degree, not in kind.

## 2C. Why choose Rs0.125? [S]

1. **Price-point engineering.** A ~2.5bn share count puts the IPO at Rs12–18, the same "feels affordable" zone as 2025–26 PSX IPOs: Zarea Rs16.5, Wahdat Rs18, Sitara Rs18.9, SLM Rs19.95. Retail investors prefer low nominal prices even though this is irrational.
2. **Retail allocation granularity.** Smaller units let more applicants get an allocation in an oversubscribed retail tranche.
3. **Liquidity.** More shares mean more trades and tighter tick-relative spreads (the stated reason for the LUCK and SYS splits).
4. **Index/float optics.** A 2.5bn-share count looks "big-cap-like" on screens.

None of these harm intrinsic value. Point 1 is mildly manipulative of investor psychology, but it is not a loophole.

## 2D. Genuine, non-economic issues created by the low par [O]

| Issue | Why it matters | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Dividend % quoting** | PSX dividends are announced as % of face value. A "**100% cash dividend**" = Rs0.125/share = **1.0% yield at Rs12.40** [C]. A "1,000%" dividend = Rs1.25 (≈10% at Rs12.40, 6.9% at Rs18) [C]. Headlines like "Khaadi announces 500% dividend" will *sound* huge but equal Rs0.625/share | Medium (misleading optics) |
| **Bonus issues become nearly costless optics** | Capitalising reserves at Rs0.125 a share uses tiny reserves, so management could announce frequent "bonus shares" that change nothing economically | Low |
| **Low nominal price → "penny-stock" perception and price-limit mechanics** | PSX daily limits have historically included a "% or Re1, whichever is higher" rule (verify the current rule). That matters if the price ever falls below ~Rs10 | Low |
| **Minimum paid-up capital optics** | Post-IPO paid-up capital is only Rs312.5mn even though equity (incl. premium) is far larger. PSX listing rules include minimum capital thresholds (current value n.v.). Paid-up capital is irrelevant to economics | Low |

## 2F. Answers to Q18–Q21

* **Economic advantage to insiders or retail from Rs0.125?** None [O]. The split is value-neutral. Any insider advantage comes from the IPO *price* (and the pre-IPO discount formula), not the par value.
* **Is "IPO price Rs18 vs face value Rs0.125" meaningful?** No. It is **misleading if used to imply overvaluation**, and equally misleading if used to imply cheapness.
* **The Rs10-equivalent IPO price** is **Rs992 (floor) to Rs1,440 (ceiling)** [C].
* **Regulatory or accounting loophole?** **No loophole found.** It is a legal capital-structure choice. The real concerns are in Section 3 (BUR agreement, carve-out of international and brand assets, related-party supply).

## 5C. What has to go right? [O]

1. **Experience Stores must lift sales per store sharply.** Average sales per store were ~Rs811mn in CY25 [C: 25,130/31, overstated because it includes e-commerce]. At ~Rs423mn capex per Experience Store [C: 3,387/8], the payback is:

| Incremental sales per converted store (Rs mn/yr) | Store EBITDA margin 15% | 20% | 25% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 | 9.4 yrs | 7.1 | 5.6 |
| 500 | 5.6 | 4.2 | 3.4 |
| 800 | 3.5 | 2.6 | 2.1 |
| 1,200 | 2.4 | 1.8 | 1.4 |

   Formula: payback = capex per store ÷ (incremental sales × margin). The strategy needs ≥ Rs800mn of incremental sales per store to pay back in under 3 years [C/A].
2. **Same-store sales growth must run well above inflation (~10%) while the policy rate rises and fuel prices spike** (Brent > US$100; [BR](https://brecorder.com/news/40443237/psx-kse-100-sheds-over-1100-points-on-selling-pressure)).
3. **Gross margin must hold ≥ 36%** despite cotton/energy cost inflation and competitors' discounting.
4. **Finance costs must stay low.** Proceeds repay the bridge loan, but the rest of the debt and the lease interest float with rates.
5. **The BUR charge must not rise.**
6. **No heavy Eid-timing distortion.** The CY27 Eids still fall in 1H (Eid al-Fitr ~Mar 2027, Eid al-Adha ~May 2027).

## 5D. What would make it fail? [O]

* Consumer slowdown from ~10% inflation plus an 11.5%+ policy rate and higher fuel prices.
* Cannibalisation: fewer, bigger stores in the same malls.
* Competition. **Sapphire Retail revenue +26% to Rs41.4bn in FY25** with 50 stores; Gul Ahmed/Ideas, Limelight (~80 stores), Nishat Linen and others are pushing hard.
* Cost inflation (rent, electricity, wages) that cannot be passed through.
* Tax changes on retail, e.g. POS sales-tax rates.
* Any increase in the BUR fee or central cost allocation.

**Typology [O]:**

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| Exploded and held | ZAL (small float, tech/agri narrative) |
| Exploded then crashed | OCTOPUS (27x oversubscribed, best of 2021, now −34%) |

Other facts:

* Rs22bn expansion to raise four-wheeler capacity from **100 to ~180 vehicles/day** in 9–10 months.

* Partnerships: **Great Wall Motor (Haval, Tank)** for the core business, plus an agreement with **BAIC** for three models (BAIC sales n.v.).
* Market cap in June 2023 ≈ **Rs2.7bn** (44.56 × ~60.4mn shares) vs ~Rs100bn now [C].

## 9B. Decomposing the return [C]

* FY23→FY24: price ×17.1 = **EPS ×7.98** × **P/E ×2.14**. In log terms, **73% of the rerating came from earnings growth** and 27% from multiple expansion.
* Jun-24 → now: price ×2.1 while EPS grew ×2.97, so **the P/E compressed from ~5.8x to ~4.1x**.
* Since the Feb-2026 peak, the price is **−36%** even though EPS kept rising. The market is now pricing peak-cycle margins, tariff liberalisation and Chinese competition.

## 9C. Was it predictable? What signals existed? [O]

**Partly yes.** The signals visible in 2023:
1. **Monthly PAMA unit sales** showing Haval volumes ramping (public, monthly data).
2. A **tiny market cap (~Rs2.7bn)** against a fast-growing new product line.
3. **Trailing P/E < 3x** with operating leverage as fixed costs were spread over higher volumes.
4. **Quarterly EPS inflections** in 1QFY24–2QFY24.
5. Import restrictions in 2022–23 that protected local assemblers (policy tailwind).
6. Products that fitted the market gap (SUV/hybrid at a price below Japanese incumbents).

**What could not be predicted:** the *magnitude* (an 8x EPS increase).

## 9D. The transferable lessons (the fundamentals, not "find another SAZEW")

1. **Earnings inflection must be visible in high-frequency data** (monthly volumes, quarterly EPS) *before* the multiple expands.
2. **Start from a low multiple and a small market cap.** The re-rating adds to EPS growth.
3. **Operating leverage plus new capacity or product** in a market with unmet demand.
4. **A policy tailwind**, which can reverse. SAZEW now faces tariff cuts and new entrants.
5. **Exit discipline matters.** Owners who bought at the Feb-2026 peak are down 36% despite record profits. A great business at a peak multiple of peak earnings is a poor investment.

# 15. BUYING STRATEGY

## 15A. Simulated comparison [C/A]

Simulation inputs: 5 years, Rs35k/month, 10% step-up.
* Path 1: steady +1.2%/month (~15%/yr).
* Path 2: same, but with a **−30% crash in months 13–18**, then +1.5%/month.
* Cash earns 10%/yr.

| Strategy | Crash path: end value (gain) | Steady path: end value (gain) |
|---|---|---|
| A. Invest every month regardless | Rs3.58mn (+39.6%) | **Rs3.67mn (+43.0%)** |
| B. Monthly, keep 20% in cash, deploy at −15% | Rs3.57mn (+39.3%) | Rs3.58mn (+39.5%) |
| C. Quarterly | **Rs3.58mn (+39.7%)** | Rs3.65mn (+42.5%) |
| D. Hold 30% back for corrections, deploy at −20% | Rs3.57mn (+39.2%) | Rs3.53mn (+37.7%) |
| E. Buy only below fair value | Depends on valuation; can leave money idle for long periods (not simulable without price data) | — |

**Interpretation [O]:**
* In a crash path, the strategies end up nearly identical.
* In a rising market, **holding cash costs you** (−3 to −5pp).
* "Waiting for corrections" only beats DCA if you can time the crash *and* actually deploy. Most people can't.

## 15B. The actionable strategy [O]

1. **Core (90%): invest monthly on a fixed date (Strategy A).**
2. **Valuation guardrail (from Strategy E):** if a stock trades **above** its "fair-value range" top, skip it that month and redirect that slice to the holding that is most below its buying range.
3. **Opportunity reserve (10%):** put 10% of each month into a money-market fund. **Deploy all of it** when the KSE-100 is ≥15% below its 52-week high, or when any core stock is ≥20% below its buying range *without a thesis-breaking event*.
4. **Reinvest all dividends.**
5. **Rebalance once a year** back to target weights. Trim any position above 30%.
6. **Review triggers, not prices:** quarterly results against the thesis (see "What would make me change my mind").

# 18. FINAL VERDICT: THE 22 QUESTIONS

**1. Is Khaadi worth subscribing to?**
**Only conditionally [O].** It is a real, profitable, recognised business with improving margins, and the IPO money genuinely goes into the company if the final prospectus confirms the issue is fully primary. But the headline cheapness comes from a forecast (PAT ×2.9 in two years) that the historical record does not yet support. The listed entity also excludes the brand IP and the international business.
* **Subscribe in the retail tranche only if the strike is ≤ ~Rs15**, and size it as a satellite (≤ 5% of your portfolio).
* At **Rs16–18, skip it.** Revisit after two quarterly results as a listed company.

**2. At what price would I consider it attractive?** **≤ Rs13.5** (≈ 8.9x my base CY27 EPS and ~20x trailing CY25 EPS) [C/O].

**3. At what price would I avoid it?** **≥ Rs17.** At Rs18 the base-case 5-year return is ~5% a year price-only (~9% with dividends), below a ~11% risk-free T-bill [C].

**4. Is the Rs0.125 face value concerning?** **No, economically [O].** It is a value-neutral subdivision. The only practical concern is that dividends quoted as "% of par" will sound much bigger than they are (100% = Rs0.125).

**5. Is there any genuine loophole or capital-structure concern?** **No loophole was found in the par value.** The genuine concerns are:
* (a) the **BUR agreement**, under which the listed company licenses the brand from its 80% parent and is allocated central costs;
* (b) **international operations excluded**, with KPL supplying related parties (transfer pricing);
* (c) the **unexplained gross-margin step-up** around the 2023–24 restructuring;
* (d) a **pre-IPO price formula** that rewards early institutions more the higher retail is made to pay;
* (e) **lock-in terms and use of the pre-IPO proceeds are n.v.**

**6. Biggest strengths:**
* A top-tier brand.
* Proven profitability: PAT Rs1.4bn → Rs1.7bn → Rs1.6bn in 1H26 alone.
* Gross margin 36–37%, operating margin 13–15%.
* Disciplined store rationalisation.
* IFC as an indirect shareholder (governance oversight at group level).
* IPO proceeds de-lever the balance sheet.

**7. Biggest weaknesses:**
* Flat nominal (falling real) revenue in 2024–25.
* Shrinking store count.
* Growth leadership lost to Sapphire.
* Brand and international upside sit with the parent.
* Profits flattered by interest-rate cuts that are now reversing.
* Forecast-dependent valuation.
* 20% float.

**8. Reasonable 2031 price?** Base **Rs20.5–25.7** under the §7 assumptions (PAT ~Rs6.4bn, 8–10x). Not a prediction; it is the implied valuation if those assumptions hold.

**9. Bear case:** CY27 PAT ~Rs2.8bn, growth 6% a year thereafter, P/E 6–7x → **Rs8.5–9.9 in 2031**. That is −45% to −53% from Rs18 before dividends.

**10. Bull case:** management's Rs4.8bn delivered in CY27, then +18% a year, P/E 11–13x → **Rs41–48 in 2031**. That is ~24–29% a year from Rs12.40–15.

**11. Five stocks with the best 5-year risk/reward:** **FFC, UBL, LUCK, SYS, HINOON** (substitutes: OGDC, MEBL, ILP).

**12. Highest upside:** **LUCK** (bull ~27% a year) and **SYS** (bull ~27% a year). **HINOON** has the highest *base-case* return (~20%).

**13. Safest:** **FFC** (7.6% yield, essential-goods demand, strong balance sheet). Runner-up: MEBL.

**14. Most undervalued:** **UBL** on P/B vs ROE (2.0x for ~31%). **OGDC** on normalised earnings (~6.8x, ex super-tax reversal).

## Appendix A: Key formulas used

* **Market cap** = shares outstanding × price
* **EV** = market cap + debt − cash (here: + assumed pre-IPO net debt − IPO proceeds; leases excluded)
* **P/E** = price ÷ EPS = market cap ÷ PAT
* **CAGR** = (end ÷ start)^(1/years) − 1
* **Justified P/E** = payout × (1 + g) ÷ (r − g)
* **Future price** = EPS₀ × (1 + g)ⁿ × exit P/E
* **Total return (approx.)** = price CAGR + average dividend yield
* **SIP value** = Σ C_t × (1 + r_m)^(N − t), with r_m = (1 + R)^(1/12) − 1 and C_t stepping up by s each year
* **Payback** = capex per store ÷ (incremental sales × store EBITDA margin)
